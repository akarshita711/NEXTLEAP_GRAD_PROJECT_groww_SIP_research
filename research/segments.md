# Candidate target segments (from the discovery engine, 4,952 relevant conversations)

How segments were built: each conversation was assigned using what the person *said about themselves* (age band, income stability, source of money). Only 1,525 of 4,952 conversations (31%) state enough to be assigned, so these are directional. Priority order for assignment: C, then A, then B, then D. Drop-off signal = paused, reduced, stopped, considering stopping, or failed instalment.

## Reading the table
- **B (salaried, fixed pay)** has the highest drop-off signal rate, with cash-flow pressure as the largest stated trigger, and needs both "is this normal?" explanation and a middle path (pause or reduce). It is a large segment and the most reachable for a student researcher (peers in early careers).
- **A (under-25s and students)** is the biggest first-year group (237 conversations) and the most confused and anxious, but shows fewer visible drop-offs. That can mean they drift silently, which the engine cannot see. Good second choice.
- **C (variable earners)** has the clearest need for flexibility (pause, skip, reduce), but is small and harder to recruit.
- **D (31+)** looks stable and goal-driven. Low priority for this metric.

## Recommendation
Primary segment: **early-career salaried first-time investors (about 22 to 30) in months 3 to 12 of their first SIP, who have wobbled** (thought of pausing, reduced, paused or missed an instalment). It overlaps B and the older half of A, matches the survey, and links to O3 with O2 as the companion.

## Limits to state in the deck
- Segment is inferred from what people choose to post. Only 31% state it.
- Drop-off counts in C and D are tiny (18 and 8).
- Public text over-represents experienced investors and people who ask questions.

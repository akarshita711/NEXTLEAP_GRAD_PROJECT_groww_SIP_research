## Personas (deck: Personas.pptx)
- **Persona 1 = R4, "Missed it, never meant to stop"**: first SIP in 2025, paid by hand, missed 2-3 instalments by forgetting, turned on autopay, did not know what several misses lead to.
- **Persona 2 = R3, "Laid off, nearly quit"**: first SIP about 4 months ago, laid off and needed money, thought of quitting over low returns, used skip and lowered the amount. Aged 34, just above the 22-30 band.

## Hypothesis check (slide 4)
First version: salaried 22-30 first-timers stop because their first doubt goes unanswered in the app, not because markets fall.

- Test 1, doubt moment + no trusted in-app answer + outside help or freezing (target 4 of 6): **2 of 4** (R2, R3). Not met.
- Test 2, a clear explanation or in-between option would have changed what they did (target 3 of 6): **4 of 4**. Met.
- "Wrong if" check: the misses and stops followed a life event (relocation, layoff) or a forgotten payment, so "not because the money ran out" is not supported.

**Validated version (on slide 4):** SIPs stop because first-timers get no proper guidance when they miss a SIP or returns are low, so they do not know what to do.
- Missed SIP: none of the 4 knew what several missed instalments lead to; R4 did nothing, asked for "a proper guide to first time SIP investors" and found GR-1 only later.
- Low returns: R3 thought of quitting over low returns and poor market knowledge; R2 found notifications too technical and was drawn to ETFs and bonds.
- All 4 named a gentler path they would have taken.
It was sharpened after the interviews, so further interviews are its independent check. Answers above are as given; none were altered.

## Notes for the MVP
- R2 and R3 asked the app to recommend funds or a better basket. That is investment advice and stays out of scope.
- R4 asked for 2-3 reminders a day and a notification that cannot be cleared. That conflicts with the no-urgency rule; a single calm reminder the day before is the safe version.

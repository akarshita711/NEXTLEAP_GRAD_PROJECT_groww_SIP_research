# AI-written headlines reviewed by hand (rejected for display)

Gemini wrote one headline per research question from the evidence pack. All nine passed the automatic number check (every number appears in the evidence). Reading them, several were still misleading, so the cards use deterministic, number-built headlines instead.

| Q | AI headline (verbatim) | Problem found on reading |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Out of 2102 items on reasons, people start a SIP for long term wealth (910 items, 43.3%) or specific goals (520 items, 24.7%). Out of 1524 items on expectations, 1121 items (73.6%) expect steady long term growth and 210 items (13.8%) expect quick returns. For no clear expectation (19 items), evidence is thin. | Accurate but wordy; items called people. |
| 2 | After starting, 271 people paused or stopped, 257 stayed consistent, 253 switched, and 232 increased amounts. In the first 3 months, evidence is thin for 569 people, with 415 starting. Between 3 and 12 months, 240 of 333 people continued. After 12 months, 1365 of 1874 people continued. | Misreads stage data: says '1365 of 1874 people continued after 12 months' and 'evidence is thin for 569 people', which are counts of items by SIP status, not people or outcomes. Numbers exist in the evidence, meaning is wrong. |
| 3 | Investors stop SIPs primarily due to understanding and expectation issues for 221 people (43.2%), followed by money or life events for 138 people (27.0%), and technical payment or app failures for 91 people (17.8%). Evidence for other triggers like advice from others is thin. | Accurate but wordy; items called people. |
| 4 | Investors most often check returns percent (263 items) and current value (244 items), while checking units (22 items) and market news (9 items) is thin. Regarding frequency, 54 investors check rarely, 40 check daily, 23 check monthly, 5 check weekly, and the evidence for checking only when prompted is thin. | Says '54 investors check rarely' from only 123 items that state a frequency, and treats items as people. |
| 5 | Users are most confused by SIP dates and status, which were mentioned 279 times (21.5%) out of 1299 items. First-year users also find SIP dates and status most confusing with 89 mentions (29.8%) out of 299 items. Overall, 887 mentions (17.9%) indicate users feel confused. | Mixes two unrelated bases: confusion mentions out of 1299 items and 'feel confused' from the whole relevant set (887). |
| 6 | When feeling unsure, 394 people most often turn to YouTube (151; 38.3%) or Reddit forums (125; 31.7%). Among 41 people showing drop-off signals, 14 use Reddit forums (34.1%) and 12 use YouTube (29.3%). Evidence for other sources like WhatsApp or Telegram is thin. | Counts items as 'people'. |
| 7 | Continuing investors prioritize steady long term growth at 28.6% compared to 14.2% for drop-offs, while drop-offs report higher feelings of confusion at 20.8% versus 11.0% for those continuing. Evidence regarding specific information sources like advisors is thin for both groups. | Compares 'steady long term growth' share across groups without saying the base is only items that state an expectation. |
| 8 | Drop-off signals vary by segment: irregular income shows 12.9% and variable pay 12.3%, while fixed salary is 9.0% and student or dependent is 5.4%. Influenced by friends or colleagues is 14.6%, family 10.5%, self research 9.2%, and creator social 7.1%. Evidence for workplace and app prompt is thin. Family allowance data is thin. | Accurate but wordy; items called people. |
| 9 | Across 4952 conversations, the most common unmet needs are plain language explanations for 769 people (15.5%) and expectation setting for 764 people (15.4%). For first-year items, the evidence for goal progress views is thin. For drop-off signals, the evidence for trusted human help and goal progress views is thin. | Calls evidence 'thin' for first-year goal progress while giving no counts; vague. |

Lesson: a number-matches-evidence check is necessary but not sufficient. The live Ask feature keeps that check (plus quote, advice and scope checks) and also tells the model to say what the base is. Display headlines on the saved cards are now built by code.
